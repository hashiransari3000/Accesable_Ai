ACCESSABLE AI - SAFETY AND LIMITATIONS
======================================

MANDATORY VISIBLE NOTICE (Dashboard + Navigation pages)
-------------------------------------------------------
"Educational prototype only. AI may miss or misidentify objects.
Do not rely on this app for safe mobility or obstacle avoidance."

1. WHAT THIS SYSTEM IS NOT
--------------------------
  - Not a certified assistive technology product.
  - Not a replacement for a white cane, guide dog, mobility training,
    or human assistance.
  - Not a medical or safety device.
  - Not capable of guaranteeing that any area is safe or free of obstacles.

2. OBJECT DETECTION LIMITATIONS
-------------------------------
  - The standard pretrained YOLO model recognizes only the classes in its
    training labels (80 COCO classes); it cannot detect everything.
  - The optional Rubik's cube face check is a classical 3x3 colour-grid
    heuristic, NOT a trained model. It is not 100% accurate: solved or
    nearly solved single-colour faces, heavy glare, blur, very small
    cubes, and occluded faces are missed. Colourful 3x3 patterns (tiles,
    windows, artwork) can produce false positives. It only runs in
    Image upload mode.
  - The optional open-vocabulary model (YOLO-World) accepts text prompts
    such as "door" or "stairs", but it is LESS reliable than fixed-class
    YOLO: scores are much lower, and unusual or abstract prompts may
    produce false positives or nothing at all. A prompt result is a
    model suggestion, never a confirmation that an object is present.
  - It can miss objects, misclassify objects, lag behind reality, or
    produce false detections.
  - Confidence is a model score, NOT a probability of safety.
  - Announcements describe the analyzed frame/image only. They are
    observations, not facts about the entire environment.
  - A 2D image does not provide reliable physical distance, obstacle
    height, floor drop-off, or collision risk. Metric distance estimation
    is out of scope; the optional proximity bands are explicitly relative
    (see section 3).

3. RELATIVE PROXIMITY (DEPTH) LIMITATIONS
-----------------------------------------
  - Optional proximity bands ("very close" ... "far") come from a
    monocular depth model (Depth Anything V2 Small). They order objects
    WITHIN one image only.
  - They are NOT metres, NOT calibrated, and NOT comparable across
    images, cameras, or moments in time.
  - Reflections, glass, very low light, unusual viewpoints, and flat
    surfaces can invert or flatten the estimate.
  - A "far" band never means "clear path"; a "very close" band never
    means "about to collide". Never use bands for obstacle avoidance.
  - If depth fails, object detections remain valid and a warning says so.

4. POSITION ESTIMATION LIMITATIONS
----------------------------------
  - "Left / center / right" refers to position within the analyzed
    image, phrased as "appears on the left side of the image". It is NOT
    a statement about the user's physical surroundings.

5. SPEECH LIMITATIONS
---------------------
  - Offline TTS voice quality depends on eSpeak NG; pronunciation of
    some words may be imperfect.
  - If TTS is unavailable, text output remains fully available.
  - Speech is user-triggered or explicitly enabled; the app never speaks
    unexpectedly on load.

6. OCR LIMITATIONS
------------------
  - English only (until other languages are explicitly tested).
  - Handwriting, very low resolution, heavy blur, extreme rotation, and
    poor lighting can cause missing or wrong text.
  - OCR output can contain errors; users should verify important text.
  - FALSE POSITIVES OCCUR: OCR can report "text" on images that contain
    none. Observed during testing: a single letter 'T'
    at 97% model confidence extracted from a text-free synthetic room
    image. Treat every extraction - especially short, high-confidence
    single characters - with suspicion.
  - "No text found" is a valid result, not an error.

7. NAVIGATION DEMO LIMITATIONS
------------------------------
  - The indoor map is a fixed, fictional sample. The system does not know
    the user's live location.
  - It does not detect stairs, doors, temporary obstacles, moving people,
    or accessible paths in a real building.
  - BFS/A* outputs are algorithm demonstrations on the supplied graph,
    never live guidance. Never described as safe real-world navigation.

8. PRIVACY BEHAVIOUR
--------------------
  - All processing happens in memory; the app writes no image or text
    data to disk. When self-hosted, processing stays on your machine; on a
    public deployment, images are processed on the hosting server.
  - No camera images, OCR text, or user data is uploaded, retained, or
    transmitted. No analytics or telemetry.
  - Model weights download once on first use (documented in
    docs/SETUP_GUIDE.txt and models/README.txt); after that the app
    works offline.

9. KNOWN FAILURE MODES
----------------------
  F1. Missing camera or denied permission -> camera mode disabled with
      fallback instructions; image upload remains available.
  F2. Model download/load failure -> clear error with retry guidance;
      rest of app stays usable.
  F3. TTS engine missing -> text summaries shown plus setup hint.
  F4. Unsupported/corrupted image -> validation error before processing.
  F5. Low memory -> OCR, open-vocabulary, and depth stay lazy-loaded;
      camera inference and auto-briefing are throttled by intervals.
  F6. Open-vocabulary or depth weights unavailable -> actionable warning;
      detections above remain valid and the rest of the app stays usable.
  F7. Rubik's cube check finds nothing -> explicit "no 3x3 colour grid"
      note (not an error); the rest of the results stay valid.

10. SAFE WORDING RULES (for the product and all documentation)
------------------------------------------------------------
  - Never say "safe", "collision prevention", or "guaranteed navigation".
  - Use "appears", "detected in this image", "based on the sample map".
  - Proximity must always be called "relative", never a distance in
    metres. Never imply that a band clearing means the path is safe.
  - The Rubik's cube check must never be described as 100% accurate or
    as a trained AI model; call it a heuristic colour-grid check.
  - Always keep the visual equivalent of spoken information available.
